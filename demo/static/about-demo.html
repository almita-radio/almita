How the ALMITA demo works

ALMITA is a home-built 21 cm hydrogen-line radio telescope. This site shows its real web console - the same pages the operator uses at night - replaying a recording. Nothing here is a real observation and nothing here moves anything.

What was recorded

ALMITA's own software (unchanged: capture, QUICKLOOK, REDUCE, SCIENCE, EXPERIMENT) ran in a container against a simulated instrument, on a simulated winter night in Santiago (15 July 2026, 22:00, the Galactic Centre overhead):

  • Mount: INDI's Telescope Simulator. ALMITA's own banner says so on every page.
  • SDR: a stand-in for rtl_tcp that speaks its protocol and streams raw I/Q in real time. The sky in that stream follows the HI4PI survey (HI4PI Collaboration 2016, A&A 594, A116) seen through ALMITA's 20° beam, Doppler-shifted for the time and place; the receiver (system temperature 120 K, band shape, noise) is a model.
  • Thermometers, Bias-T, the capture Pi: simulated too.

A browser then pressed the buttons: PLAN and START on OBSERVE (a 5 × 5 grid around galactic longitude 10°, with frequency switching), MONITOR while it ran, REDUCE with the v2 estimator, SCIENCE maps, SESSIONS, and an EXPERIMENT recipe. Every request and every answer was recorded.

What you are seeing

A service worker in your browser answers the pages' requests from that recording. When you press a button that was pressed during the recording (START, RUN), the recording plays from that moment, ten times faster. Buttons that were not recorded say "disabled in the SIMULATED demo". The clock on the pages is the simulated night's.

What it is not

Not ALMITA's data, not a measurement, and not a test of the hardware: the sky is a survey, the receiver a model. The real results - and their honest caveats - are on the project page.
